EARTH Beat

STYLED AFTER NASA SVS 5313
2023

Hear pictures of the Earth. Each layer is played as music you can turn by hand. Made for blind and low-vision listeners, and for everyone else.

How to use it

  • Drag the globe, or use the arrow keys, to turn it.
  • Each yellow ring is a latitude band. The part of the band facing you sets the note.
  • Press Play to start the music. The year control works on Night Lights and CO₂.

The layers

  • Lightning: ISS Lightning Imaging Sensor, 2017–2023 (SVS 5011). It sees only the tropics and mid-latitudes, up to about 38° north and south. Sounds as clicks.
  • Night Lights: NASA Black Marble, 2012–2023 (SVS 5313). Purple lights grew, white are unchanged, orange faded. Growth rings out as rising bright notes, fading as falling low notes.
  • Ocean: sea surface temperature from the ECCO2 model (SVS 3912). Warmer water sounds stronger.
  • Currents: Gulf Stream, Kuroshio, Agulhas and other western boundary currents (SVS 3912, 5425). Sounds as glides.
  • Aerosols: GEOS model for 19 August 2025 (SVS 5669). Sea salt is blue, dust magenta, smoke orange and sulfate green. A dark drone.
  • CO₂: GEOS model for January to March 2020 (SVS 5196). The level follows NOAA’s global yearly mean, 394 ppm in 2012 to 421 ppm in 2023, and the low pulse rises in pitch and strength with it.
  • Forests: GEDI canopy height, 2019–2020 (SVS 4950). GEDI sees only between 51.6° north and south. Short high plucks.
  • Moisture: an atmospheric river reaching the US west coast (SVS 4960). Sounds as glides.
  • Warming: NASA GISS GISTEMP, the global land-ocean temperature anomaly, one note for every year from 1880 to 2015. Cooler years sound lower and warmer years higher, with a slow bass line following the five-year average. Press Play to hear 135 years in about 40 seconds.

Only Night Lights and CO₂ use the year control. Warming steps through its own years. The other layers are single snapshots, so they stay the same whatever the year.

Warming plays NASA GISS’s published yearly temperature numbers. The other layers are re-creations with simplified, synthetic data placed by hand, not NASA imagery or measurements.
